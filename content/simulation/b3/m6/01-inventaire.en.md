On 31 December the year stops: count, value, depreciate [[syscohada:6031]].

### Thursday 31 December: the 31 December count

On the evening of the 31st the warehouse closes to customers. Didier and Serge count every full crate, row by row: 600 crates of 65 cl and 240 of 33 cl. The physical count matches the stock card kept every evening.

[[piece:S-B3-12-010]]

**The rule.** Periodic inventory: purchases go to 6011 all year; on 31 December closing stock at weighted average cost goes into 3111 and 3112, through 6031.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why WAC rather than FIFO? Both are allowed, but one must be chosen and kept. With July's price rise FIFO would give a higher closing stock, hence a higher profit: the contrast exercise measures it.

**What it changes.** Closing stock of 5,485,200 FCFA reduces the year's cost of sales.

**Where it is filed.** Signed count sheets and WAC card in the Inventory binder; the inventory summary goes into the inventory book. Entry in the miscellaneous journal (OD).

**See again:** Accounting M20 “Inventories at year end”.

### Thursday 31 December: depreciation

Serge opens the asset register: the truck, in use since February, and the crates bought in January. He computes 3,300,000 FCFA for the truck (11 months) and 200,000 FCFA for the crates.

[[piece:S-B3-12-011]]

**The rule.** Charges 6813; accumulated depreciation 2845 and 2843, straight-line over five years, pro rata.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why five years? It is the useful life the company chose. Tax law sets maximum rates (33.33% for a truck): the 20% book rate stays below, nothing to add back.

**What it changes.** 3,500,000 FCFA of expenses, with no cash going out.

**Where it is filed.** Asset register printed and filed in the Fixed-asset binder. Entry in the miscellaneous journal (OD).

**See again:** Accounting M18 “Depreciation”.
