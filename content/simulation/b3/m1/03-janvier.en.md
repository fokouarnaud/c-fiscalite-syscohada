January 2026: the depot opens. Read each scene, look at the document it produces, then record the entry in the quiz [[cgi:art. 142]].

### Friday 2 January: the capital goes to the bank

Rose Ateba and her partner pay 10,000,000 FCFA to Bank X, into an account in the name of the company being formed. The notary records the deposit in a declaration of subscription and payment, and the funds stay blocked until the company is entered in the trade register.

[[piece:S-B3-01-006]]

**The rule.** Subscribed, called and paid-up capital is credited to 1013; the bank 521 is debited.

> **Why this choice and not another.** A beginner sees “income”: it is not revenue, nothing was sold. Nor is it a debt to Rose (4621): capital stays in the company and is rewarded by dividends, not repaid.

**What it changes.** The bank shows 10,000,000 FCFA, available once the company is registered; profit does not change.

**Where it is filed.** In the permanent file, with the articles and the notarised declaration, kept for the company's whole life. Entry in the bank journal (BQ).

**See again:** Accounting M3 “Assets and liabilities”.

### Monday 5 January: the opening-stock invoice

Since the 2nd, the brewery's trucks have made several runs a day to fill the warehouse: 3,570 full crates in all. Didier counts each load and signs the delivery notes; Mr Fouda sums them up on one invoice on the 5th. Bottles and crates still belong to the brewery, which charges a deposit on them.

[[piece:S-B3-01-011]]

![The crate and deposit circuit](/static/content/simulation/b3/circuit-casiers.en.svg)

**The rule.** Purchases in 6011, excise included; VAT in 4452; withholding in 4492; deposit in 4094.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Three traps. Excise has no account of its own at the depot: the brewer pays the 25% duty and the additional specific duty (75 per 65 cl bottle) and includes both in its price; the invoice shows only the latter, for information. The deposit is not a purchase: it is a receivable refunded when the empties go back. The withholding is not an expense: it is an advance on tax, credited against the CIT instalment.

**What it changes.** The debt to the brewery is 40,763,313 FCFA, payable at the end of next month; 13,476,000 FCFA of deposits wait for the empties.

**Where it is filed.** Invoice in the Purchases binder, stapled to the delivery notes signed by Didier; the same notes feed the stock card every evening. Entry in the purchases journal (AC) on the day the invoice arrives.

**See again:** Accounting M6 “Purchases”; Taxation M4 “VAT and excise duties”.

### Tuesday 6 January: the warehouse rent

The depot moves into a rented warehouse in Mvog-Ada. Serge pays the 250,000 FCFA rent but hands Mrs Owona only 225,000 FCFA: he keeps the rest for the tax office.

[[piece:S-B3-01-010]]

**The rule.** A tenant under the actual regime withholds tax on rent (art. 87) and pays it over by the 15th: gross rent in 6222, withholding in 4478.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Booking only the net is wrong: the expense is the gross rent, and the withholding is a debt to the State, not a saving.

**What it changes.** 25,000 FCFA go into the 15 February return.

**Where it is filed.** Receipt in the Bank and Mobile Money binder with the transfer order; the original lease goes to the permanent file. Entry in the bank journal (BQ); Serge notes the withholding for the 15th return.

**See again:** Taxation M3 “Personal income tax”.

### Tuesday 6 January: the articles at the notary's

With ten million of capital the LLC is above the one-million threshold, so a notary drafts its articles. The company is now registered and the funds released: Serge pays Maître Abena's invoice for fees, VAT and the registration duties he advanced.

[[piece:S-B3-01-007]]

**The rule.** Fees in 6324, registration duties in 646, recoverable VAT on services in 4454.

> **Why this choice and not another.** The old reflex is to capitalise them as formation expenses. Revised SYSCOHADA removed that line: they are expenses of the year, by nature.

**What it changes.** The first year starts with an expense; the VAT on fees is deducted on 15 February.

**Where it is filed.** Invoice in the permanent file, as proof of the set-up costs. Entry in the purchases journal (AC).

**See again:** Accounting M1 “The OHADA accounting framework”.

### Tuesday 6 January: crates marked Ateba

So as not to depend on the brewery's crates alone, Rose orders 400 empty crates marked with the depot's name. They will circulate for years between the warehouse and the bars.

[[piece:S-B3-01-008]]

**The rule.** Returnable, identifiable, durable packaging is a fixed asset: 243, VAT on fixed assets 4451, depreciated at year end.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Not 6082: these crates are not used up within the year. Not 2493 either, despite its label: that line is for assets in progress, not crates in service.

**What it changes.** Assets grow by one million; the expense comes through depreciation over five years.

**Where it is filed.** Invoice in the Fixed-asset binder, copy in the permanent file; the crates join the software's asset register. Entry in the purchases journal (AC).

**See again:** Accounting M13 “Fixed assets”.

### Tuesday 20 January: the Rond-Point order

Mrs Ngah gets her snack bar ready for the month-end evening and orders 150 crates, payable on the 10th of next month. She has no empties yet, so the depot charges her a deposit per crate.

[[piece:S-B3-01-012]]

**The rule.** Sale in 7011, VAT charged 4431, customer 411; the deposit goes to 4194, outside the VAT base for identifiable packaging (art. 139).

> **Why this choice and not another.** The deposit is not a sale: the depot must refund it when the empties come back. Booking it in 7011 would inflate turnover, the CIT instalment and VAT.

**What it changes.** Mrs Ngah owes 1,810,953 FCFA, of which 576,000 FCFA is deposit.

**Where it is filed.** Copy of the invoice in the Sales binder, in number order with no gaps; Didier enters the deposited crates in the empties book under Mrs Ngah. Entry in the sales journal (VT).

**See again:** Accounting M7 “Sales”.

### Saturday 31 January: the counter till

All month, local bars came to the counter, dropped their empties and left with full crates, paid in cash. Didier closes the till on the evening of the 31st.

[[piece:S-B3-01-013]]

**The rule.** Cash 5711 incl. tax, sales 7011 excl. tax, VAT 4431.

> **Why this choice and not another.** No deposit here: empties are swapped for full crates on the spot, nothing is owed either way.

**What it changes.** 17,057,520 FCFA are in the till; the VAT collected adds to the 15th return.

**Where it is filed.** Every evening Didier counts the cash and enters it in the cash book, signed with Serge; the monthly summary goes to the Cash binder. Entry in the cash journal (CA).

**See again:** Accounting M7 “Sales”.

### Saturday 31 January: the Orange Money statement

Other bars pay by Orange Money from their phones. At month end the operator sends its statement: receipts, fees deducted, then the balance transferred to the bank.

[[piece:S-B3-01-014]]

**The rule.** Sub-account 552 (5522 Orange Money), fees in 631, transfer through 585.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Fees do not reduce the sale: they are a service, like bank charges. The transfer never goes straight from 5522 to 521: it waits in 585 until the bank receives it.

**What it changes.** 10,128,227 FCFA leave for the bank.

**Where it is filed.** Statement downloaded from the merchant area, printed and filed in the Bank and Mobile Money binder. Entry in the Orange Money journal (OM).

**See again:** Accounting M12 “Settlements”.

### Saturday 31 January: the first payroll

Serge prepares pay for Didier, for Paul (hired ahead of the truck) and for himself. The payslip gives the CNPS contributions; Serge computes income tax, council cents, the audiovisual fee, local tax and the housing levy.

[[piece:S-B3-01-015]]

**The rule.** Gross pay 6611, employer levies 6413 and 6641; tax withheld 4472, CNPS 431, net pay 422.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Withholdings are not the company's expenses: they belong to the employees and the depot pays them over to the State and the CNPS. The expense is gross pay plus employer contributions.

**What it changes.** 595,013 FCFA are paid to staff; withholdings go into the 15th return.

**Where it is filed.** Signed payslips in the Payroll binder, a copy handed to each employee. Entry in the miscellaneous journal (OD).

**See again:** Accounting M11 “Payroll”; Taxation M3 “Personal income tax”.

### Saturday 31 January: the takings go to the bank

Keeping millions in cash in a warehouse is risky: every week Didier takes the takings to the bank and brings back a slip stamped by the cashier. On the 31st Serge gathers the month's slips and sums them up.

[[piece:S-B3-01-016]]

![The depot's three tills and the internal transfer](/static/content/simulation/b3/trois-caisses.en.svg)

**The rule.** Internal transfer: 585 debit, cash 5711 credit; the bank debits 521 and clears 585 on receipt.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Debiting 521 straight from the till works once, then breaks: the cash slip and the bank statement would each record the deposit, counting it twice. 585 keeps one entry per document.

**What it changes.** 17,057,520 FCFA leave the till, which returns to zero.

**Where it is filed.** Slips stamped by the bank, stapled to the cash book in the Cash binder. Entry in the cash journal (CA).

**See again:** Accounting M12 “Settlements”.

### Saturday 31 January: the bank statement

January's statement arrives: salaries paid, deposits received, account fees. Serge ticks it line by line against account 521.

[[piece:S-B3-01-009]]

**The rule.** Each statement line clears the account waiting for it: 422 for salaries, 585 for internal transfers; fees go to 631.

> **Why this choice and not another.** A gap between the statement and 521 is not “fixed” by forcing the account: it is explained (an item not yet recorded on one side), then the missing entry is booked.

**What it changes.** The 521 balance at 31 January is the one learners compute in the month's quiz.

**Where it is filed.** Statement in the Bank and Mobile Money binder, with the reconciliation signed by Serge. Entries in the bank journal (BQ), then reconciliation in the software before closing the month.

**See again:** Accounting M29 “Organisation and accounting controls”.

### January in brief

- A contribution is not revenue: capital goes to 1013.
- A deposit is neither a purchase nor a sale: receivable 4094 with the brewery, debt 4194 to the customer.
- Excise is in the purchase price; the withholding is an advance on tax.
- Withholdings (rent, pay) are debts to the State, not expenses.
- Everything moving between the till, Orange Money and the bank goes through 585.
