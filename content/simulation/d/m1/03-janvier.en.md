January 2026: Jean-Claude Tchinda sets up on his own in Biyem-Assi. Read each scene, then record the entry in the quiz [[cgi:art. 134]].

### Friday 2 January: the owner's contributions

Jean-Claude assigns to the workshop the van he owned, valued at 6,000,000 FCFA by an assessor, and deposits 3,000,000 FCFA of savings in the business account.

[[piece:S-D-01-006]]

**The rule.** Personal capital 103; van 2451, bank 521.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why 103 and not 101? There is no company and no partners: share capital does not exist. What the owner contributes remains his, but the workshop tracks it in its books.

**What it changes.** The workshop starts with a vehicle and cash; no revenue.

**Where it is filed.** Permanent file, with the assessor's report on the van. Journal OD.

**See again:** Accounting M3 “Assets and liabilities”.

### Monday 5 January: the tools

He buys a gauge set, a vacuum pump and a leak detector: 450,000 FCFA before tax, paid by transfer.

[[piece:S-D-01-007]]

**The rule.** Small tools 6056, VAT 4454.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why an expense and not a fixed asset? Below the 500,000 FCFA threshold, small tools are expensed; Tech Clim, above it, had capitalised them.

**What it changes.** A first-month expense and a VAT credit.

**Where it is filed.** Invoice in the Purchases binder. Journal AC.

**See again:** Accounting M13 “Fixed assets”.

### Monday 5 January: the workshop rent

Jean-Claude pays Mrs Onana the workshop rent, less 15,000 FCFA withheld for tax.

[[piece:S-D-01-009]]

**The rule.** Gross rent 6222, withholding 4478.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Under the actual regime the tenant withholds and pays over: the expense remains the gross rent.

**What it changes.** The withholding joins the 15th return.

**Where it is filed.** Receipt in the Bank binder; lease in the permanent file. Journal BQ.

**See again:** Taxation M3 “Personal income tax”.

### Tuesday 6 January: restocking refrigerant and parts

Mrs Mvondo delivers the month's R410A cylinders and parts kits.

[[piece:S-D-01-010]]

**The rule.** Consumables 6041, VAT 4452, 2% withholding 4492.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why 6041 and not 6011? Jean-Claude does not resell these parts as such: he uses them in jobs he invoices as services.

**What it changes.** Debt to the supplier: 3,831,500 FCFA, payable at the end of next month.

**Where it is filed.** Invoice in the Purchases binder with the delivery note. Journal AC.

**See again:** Accounting M6 “Purchases”.

### Saturday 24 January: repairs for businesses

Jean-Claude and Brice made 38 call-outs at Résidence hôtelière Les Manguiers (fictive). The invoice goes with the signed job sheets, payable on the 10th of next month.

[[piece:S-D-01-011]]

**The rule.** Services 706, customer 411; VAT pending 471, due on collection.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why 471 and not 4432? For services VAT is due only on collection: until the customer pays, it is not yet a debt to the State, though it is already invoiced.

**What it changes.** A receivable on the customer.

**Where it is filed.** Invoice copy in the Sales binder with the signed job sheets. Journal VT.

**See again:** Accounting M7 “Sales”; Taxation M4 “VAT and excise duties”.

### Monday 26 January: the public maintenance contract

This month 80 units serviced for Commune d'arrondissement de Yaoundé VI (fictive). The contract stays under 5 million before tax.

[[piece:S-D-01-012]]

**The rule.** Same entry as for a private customer: 411, 706, 471.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why not deduct withholdings now? They arise on payment, when the Treasury makes them; on invoicing Jean-Claude claims the full amount.

**What it changes.** A receivable on the public payer, paid next month.

**Where it is filed.** Invoice copy in the Sales binder with the signed job sheets. Journal VT.

**See again:** Accounting M7 “Sales”.

### Saturday 31 January: home repairs

Households paid 918,225 FCFA by Orange Money; the operator takes its fees and the balance goes to the bank.

[[piece:S-D-01-013]]

**The rule.** 552, 706; invoice and payment the same day: 471 cleared to 4432 at once; fees 631, transfer through 585.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why go through 471 if payment is immediate? The software applies one rule to all services: VAT waits in 471 and falls due on payment, here the same day.

**What it changes.** VAT on these repairs is due this month.

**Where it is filed.** Printed statement in the Bank and Mobile Money binder. Journal OM.

**See again:** Accounting M12 “Settlements”; Taxation M4 “VAT and excise duties”.

### Saturday 31 January: Brice's pay

Jean-Claude prepares Brice's payslip and transfers 115,464 FCFA net.

[[piece:S-D-01-014]]

**The rule.** Gross 6611, levies, withholdings, net 422.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Brice is an employee; Jean-Claude has no payslip: one does not employ oneself.

**What it changes.** Withholdings join the 15th return.

**Where it is filed.** Signed payslip in the Payroll binder. Journal OD.

**See again:** Accounting M11 “Payroll”.

### Saturday 31 January: the bank statement

The statement shows the private customer's transfer, the supplier's payment, the wage, the Orange Money transfer and the 300,000 FCFA Jean-Claude moves to his personal account for his household.

[[piece:S-D-01-008]]

**The rule.** 411 and VAT due, 401, 422, 585, drawings 1042, 631.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why is the personal transfer not a wage? The owner is paid through profit; what he takes during the year is an advance on it, in the owner's account, never an expense.

**What it changes.** 521 balance at January 31: 2,821,636 FCFA.

**Where it is filed.** Statement and reconciliation in the Bank binder. Journal BQ.

**See again:** Accounting M29 “Organisation and accounting controls”.

### January in brief

- VAT on unpaid invoices pending in 471.
