January 2026: the company starts with a hundred thousand francs of capital and a partner's advance. Under the cash-basis system we follow the money in and out [[cgi:art. 93 quater]].

### Saturday 3 January: the minimum capital

Hervé and Mr Etoga deposit 100,000 FCFA: 20 shares of 5,000 FCFA, 16 for Mr Etoga, 4 for Hervé. Below one million of capital, a notary is optional.

[[piece:S-E-01-005]]

**The rule.** Under the cash basis it is a cash receipt that is not revenue: a contribution.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Capital is not part of the IGS turnover: only sales and services count.

**What it changes.** The bank receives the first funds.

**Where it is filed.** Permanent file; one line in the book.

**See again:** Accounting M3 “Assets and liabilities”.

### Monday 5 January: Mr Etoga's advance

A hundred thousand francs is not enough to start. Mr Etoga lends the company 1,400,000 FCFA, repayable from July.

[[piece:S-E-01-006]]

**The rule.** Partner current account 4621: a debt of the company to Mr Etoga.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why an advance rather than more capital? Capital stays locked in the company; an advance is repaid when cash allows.

**What it changes.** In the book: a receipt that is neither a sale nor revenue.

**Where it is filed.** Permanent file; one line in the book.

**See again:** Accounting M15 “Financing”.

### Monday 5 January: the month's air conditioners

Hervé buys split air conditioners for resale. The supplier, under the actual regime, charges VAT and a 26,000 FCFA withholding because the company is under the IGS.

[[piece:S-E-01-008]]

**The rule.** 5% withholding for an IGS customer; VAT not recoverable.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why 5% and not 2%? The 2% rate is for actual-regime buyers; an IGS taxpayer suffers 5%. Why count it in the cost? The CGI credits it only against corporate or personal income tax; the IGS offers no credit, so the company bears it, like VAT.

**What it changes.** A 646,100 FCFA payment.

**Where it is filed.** Payments binder; one line in the receipts and payments book.

**See again:** Taxation M1 “The tax system and regimes”.

### Tuesday 6 January: the tools

Hervé buys a vacuum pump, a manifold and a leak detector from Clim Distribution Wouri, paid by transfer.

[[piece:S-E-01-007]]

**The rule.** Small tools as an expense 6056, under the 500,000 threshold; under the IGS, VAT charged is part of the cost.

> **Why this choice and not another.** An IGS taxpayer does not charge VAT, so it does not recover any: the payment is the price incl. tax.

**What it changes.** A 477,000 FCFA payment in the book.

**Where it is filed.** Payments binder; one line in the receipts and payments book.

**See again:** Taxation M1 “The tax system and regimes”.

### Saturday 10 January: a job at Hôtel Akwa Palace (fictif)

Hervé services Hôtel Akwa Palace (fictif)'s air conditioners; the invoice, without VAT, is paid by transfer.

[[piece:S-E-01-009]]

**The rule.** Service 7061; invoice without VAT under the IGS.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Why no VAT? The IGS discharges the business licence and VAT: the company pays instead a yearly amount set by its turnover class.

**What it changes.** A 450,000 FCFA receipt, part of the IGS turnover.

**Where it is filed.** Receipts binder; one line in the receipts and payments book.

**See again:** Taxation M1 “The tax system and regimes”.

### Monday 26 January: training the customers

On Saturdays Mr Etoga trains customers' staff to use the units. As a civil servant he cannot be an employee: he issues a fee note, paid in cash.

[[piece:S-E-01-012]]

**The rule.** Fees 632, not wages.

> **Why this choice and not another.** An employment contract is in principle barred for a civil servant; a fee pays for a one-off service, with no payslip and no CNPS.

**What it changes.** A 60,000 FCFA payment.

**Where it is filed.** Payments binder; one line in the receipts and payments book.

**See again:** Accounting M10 “Other expenses and income”.

### Saturday 31 January: cash sales

Private customers buy units for cash: 750,000 FCFA in the month.

[[piece:S-E-01-011]]

**The rule.** Goods sold 7011, in cash 5711.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Under the cash basis the receipt is entered the day the money comes in, not the day of the order.

**What it changes.** The cash is deposited at the bank the next month.

**Where it is filed.** Receipts binder; one line in the receipts and payments book.

**See again:** Accounting M7 “Sales”.

### Saturday 31 January: the Orange Money statement

The Orange Money statement shows the customer's payment and the transfer to the bank, less 3,000 FCFA of fees.

[[piece:S-E-01-013]]

**The rule.** Sub-account 552 (5522), transfer through 585, fees 631.

> **Why this choice and not another.** A transfer between Orange Money and the bank is neither a receipt nor a payment: money only changes pockets.

**What it changes.** Only the fees are a payment in the book.

**Where it is filed.** Bank and Mobile Money binder, with the book ticked.

**See again:** Accounting M12 “Settlements”.

### Saturday 31 January: the bank statement

The statement shows the Orange Money transfer, the cash deposit and the fees. Hervé ticks it line by line.

[[piece:S-E-01-014]]

**The rule.** Bank 521.

> **Why this choice and not another.** Ticking reveals anything missing from the book.

**What it changes.** The month's cash position is closed.

**Where it is filed.** Bank and Mobile Money binder, with the book ticked.

**See again:** Accounting M29 “Organisation and accounting controls”.

### January in brief

- Receipts and payments entered, statements ticked.

Reminder: no VAT return under the IGS; the tax is settled with the yearly return.
