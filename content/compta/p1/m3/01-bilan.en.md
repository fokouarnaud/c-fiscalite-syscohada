## The balance sheet, a snapshot of the business

The balance sheet shows, at a given date, what the business **holds** (assets, or uses of funds) and how it is **funded** (liabilities and equity, or sources of funds). Both totals are always equal [[guide:3e partie]].

| Assets (uses) | Liabilities and equity (sources) |
|---|---|
| Fixed assets: long-lived assets, class 2 | Equity: capital, reserves, result, class 1 |
| Current assets: inventories and receivables, classes 3 and 4 | Financial debts: borrowings, class 1 |
| Cash assets: bank and cash, class 5 | Current liabilities: suppliers, State, staff, class 4 |
| | Cash liabilities: overdrafts, class 5 |

## Equity = assets − debts

What remains when all debts are taken from the assets belongs to the owners: that is equity.

## Example: Mbarga Distribution at start-up

The partners pay 5,000,000 CFA francs into the bank account ([[syscohada:521]]) and contribute a delivery vehicle valued at 8,000,000 ([[syscohada:245]]). The bank lends 10,000,000 ([[syscohada:162]]).

| Assets | Amount | Liabilities and equity | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transport equipment | 8,000,000 | Share capital | 13,000,000 |
| Bank | 15,000,000 | Bank loan | 10,000,000 |
| **Total** | **23,000,000** | **Total** | **23,000,000** |
